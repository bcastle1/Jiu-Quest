# Unreal renderer pilot inside JiuQuest

The purchased-gi MetaHuman now has a separately verified local Win64 standalone idle/walk preview. It is not the paired-character Editor receiver described below and does not yet advertise combat moves. The two DGX Sparks have authenticated hardware inventories; their installed-software compatibility gaps and the remaining release checks are recorded in [Unreal Spark hosting](unreal-spark-hosting.md).

The experimental [native character receiver](../unreal/native-runtime/README.md) separates video from local playback commands. Its `jiuquest.characterPreview` channel is limited to the human idle/walk study. It is not connected to this app's combat bridge, and importing its source does not start a listener or change the production renderer. See its validation table for the exact compiled, tested and still-pending boundaries.

The local development app can select an Unreal Pixel Streaming preview in its actual Training and Arena views. This is restricted to a Vite development build at `http://127.0.0.1:5178`; use `?engine=unreal` or the local **Character graphics** selector. Production renderer selection is unchanged. The local iframe loads the pinned Epic UE5.8 library from the loopback service described in `docs/unreal-local-streaming.md`. This integration does not supply a public GPU host. A working Unreal receiver, actual imported characters, controls and presented video must be verified together; protocol tests alone do not prove rendering.

## Public character preview (captured stills)

jiuquest.com shows three captured stills of the saved purchased-gi character at `/?preview=unreal`, linked from the Overview. Per the local capture receipt recorded November 10, 2026, they are unedited native viewport captures from the verified character-control scene: white gi standing, a face and collar close-up, and navy gi during the walk animation. `src/unreal/characterPreview.js` pins each file's size and SHA-256, and `tests/unrealCharacterPreview.test.js` fails if a file is replaced or re-encoded. Change the pinned values only together with a newly verified capture.

The page is a static gallery. It never mounts the game, reads or writes saved progress, opens a stream, or changes renderer selection; the production combat renderer remains Unity. `tools/test_unreal_character_preview.mjs` checks the built page in a browser: image decoding, view selection, phone layouts and the absence of saved-progress writes. That check covers still images only. It is not evidence of Unreal rendering, browser video or paired choreography, which remain the separate runtime checks described below.

## Unreal athlete in the Fighter Creator (tintable stills)

The Fighter Creator offers two looks for a fighter: **Unreal athlete** and **Classic**. The Unreal athlete is the purchased-gi character as a front-view still. It appears on the profile stage, in the round portraits (the top bar, the Overview's roster and the player's card in the Arena scoreboard), beside the proficiency test in Training, on the rank path in Insights, and in the Overview hero when the hero is at least 700 pixels wide and the page is wider than 760 pixels (otherwise the hero shows the `standing_stance` poster instead; see the narrow hero below). The position art of the Arena and Training is other art. For the standing position a player with the Unreal athlete sees the Unreal standing still (see Unreal position stills below). Every other position picture is the classic art. The classic pictures and the Unity scene cannot show the Unreal athlete, so they draw such a player with a stand-in look taken from the chosen head (see the next section).

The picture is four still layers that the browser adds together:

    picture = base + jacket x giTop + pants x giPants + belt x beltColor

The athlete stays dark until the body, the picture of a chosen head and all three gi layers have loaded, so a gi never shows without the person wearing it; a layer that fails to load does not hold the others back. The next section says what stands in when a picture of a chosen head cannot load.

`tools/build_unreal_athlete_layers.py` derives the layers from three aligned renders in the local `Unreal-render-pack/athlete-01/front` set: white gi, black gi, and black gi with a white belt. The render receipt of November 11, 2026 records them as unedited 2400 by 3000 native renders of the hip-belt variant `BP_AthleteGi_HipBelt`, in which the belt sits on the hips. The published layers are 900 by 1760. They are derived images, not direct renders. With every color white the sum gives back the white-gi render (mean difference 0.013 of 255 on the gi). Every other color is computed rather than rendered, and there is no rendered reference for a jacket and pants in different colors, because the current materials color the whole gi at once; the tool separates the two at the jacket hem it finds in the white-gi render. The belt takes the rank color; stripes are not drawn on the athlete. The pack also holds side, back and portrait renders, which the site does not use yet.

`src/unreal/athlete.js` pins the SHA-256 of the three source renders and the size and SHA-256 of each published layer, and `tests/unrealAthlete.test.js` fails if a layer is replaced or re-encoded or if the tool and the page disagree about the sources. Replace the layers only by rerunning the tool on newly verified renders and updating the pinned values together. The first published layers (November 11, 2026, #24) came from four lower-resolution captures in `Unreal-gi-color-preview` with the belt at the waist; the still preview at `/?preview=unreal` still shows captures of that earlier variant.

There is one athlete build, in one pose, with a choice of heads: face, hairstyle, hair color and beard (see the next section). While it is selected the Fighter Creator hides the classic gender, hair, skin tone and patch emblem choices, and keeps their saved values for the way back to Classic. The two buttons of the look picker say what each look offers: "Rendered in Unreal Engine. Face, hair, beard and gi colors." and "Original art. Skin tone, hairstyle, emblem and gi colors." New fighters and first visits start as the Unreal athlete. A fighter saved before this change moves to the Unreal athlete only if it still has the stock face, hair, skin and emblem; a fighter someone designed keeps its classic art until its owner switches.

`tools/test_unreal_athlete.mjs` checks the built site in a browser: layer decoding and blending, painted colors after each choice, the mat under the athlete (see below), both looks, saved fighters, reload, and 390 and 320 pixel layouts. Like the preview check, it covers still images only and is not evidence of Unreal rendering, browser video or paired choreography. Selecting the athlete never opens a stream and is not an input to renderer selection.

## Faces, hair and beards of the Unreal athlete

A player who chose the Unreal athlete also chooses its head in the Fighter Creator: one of two faces, one of five hairstyles, one of four hair colors, and a beard or none. `src/unreal/athleteHeadOptions.js` lists the choices and is the one list the page, the tests and the picture tool read. The table gives each option as its button names it, with the id that is saved in brackets:

| Choice | Options | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face | Standard (`original`), Dean (`dean`) | The athlete of the first renders; the MetaHuman preset "Dean" from Fab, built on the athlete's body. |
| Hair | Side-swept fringe (`fringe`); Buzz cut, skin fade (`skin-fade`); Short coily, low fade (`coily-fade`); Coil buzz cut (`coil-buzz`); Shaved (`shaved`) | The first athlete's hair (the fringe) and Dean's own hair (the coil buzz cut), both from the MetaHuman library; the two grooms bought on Fab (PinkNinjasStudio's Buzzcut Skin Fade, KhitrovArt's Short Coily Buzz Cut with Low Shadow Fade); and no hair. |
| Hair color | Black (`black`), Brown (`brown`), Blond (`blond`), Grey (`grey`) | Parameters of the hair material (melanin, redness and a dye color), written to the hair, the beard and, more lightly, the eyebrows. |
| Beard | Clean-shaven (`none`), Stubble (`stubble`) | The stubble beard and moustache Dean comes with. |

The Standard face is the one every clip shows, whatever a player chose, so its button carries the line "As in the clips" under its name.

A shaved head without a beard has no hair to color, so its four hair colors are one picture. That leaves 74 different heads, 37 for each face. The four choices are fighter fields (`athleteFace`, `athleteHair`, `athleteHairColor`, `athleteBeard`), saved with the fighter like the gi colors. A fighter saved before they existed, a new fighter and any unknown value give the published athlete: the Standard face, the fringe, black hair, clean-shaven. The classic hair and skin fields are other fields and never choose an Unreal head. The other way round, the head does choose a classic look for the pictures that cannot show the Unreal athlete, without saving it (see the stand-in look below).

**In the Fighter Creator.** `src/components/AthleteHeadPicker.jsx` stands under the look picker. Face and Hair are buttons with a 128 by 128 picture each: each face with the chosen hairstyle, and each hairstyle on the chosen face, always with black hair and no beard. Hair Color is four swatches, and Beard is two buttons with text, side by side where each name fits on one line and one above the other where it would not. The labels of the four groups are set like the labels of the fields around them, and the buttons answer a press like the other buttons of the page (`src/game/motion.css`).

The hair colors stay in use for a shaved head without a beard. The swatches look and work as always (dimmed, their outlines were too faint to find). A color pressed there is saved, and it shows as soon as the athlete has hair or a beard. The note "Shows once you add hair or a beard." stands under the swatches and is tied to each of them for screen readers (`aria-describedby`).

On a page up to 760 pixels wide the Fighter Creator is one column, and the stage has scrolled out of sight by the time these buttons are reached. The pictures on the buttons show neither the hair color nor the beard. So a live copy of the athlete's head sits beside Hair Color and Beard there (`.athlete-head-preview`): the same head-and-collar square as the round portraits, hidden from screen readers. On pages narrower than 360 pixels it stands above the two groups. Pages wider than 760 pixels do not show it, because the stage stands beside the buttons there.

A note under the look picker tells the player where the look shows: "Choose this athlete's face, hair and beard, then the gi colors below. The belt follows your rank. Your portrait and the Unreal standing picture show this look in Training and the Arena. Clips and stance pictures show the standard athlete, and classic position pictures follow your hair color and haircut."

**Every head is an Unreal render, not an edit of one.** Each of the 74 was rendered in Unreal Engine with the published scene: the same body, pose, camera, light and black gi, and only the head exchanged. The page then shows the picture it already showed, with three changes (`src/unreal/athleteHeads.js`):

- The base picture is the published base for the Standard face. Dean has a base picture of his own, because another skin also shows on the hands, the feet and the chest.
- Dean also has a jacket layer of his own. It is the published jacket layer everywhere except around the head: his neck is slimmer than the first athlete's, so more of the collar shows beside it, and the published layer has no light there (with a white gi the collar beside his neck would stay black). The piece around his head is the light a white gi adds in a render of Dean himself.
- When the chosen head is not the one its face's base shows, a small picture with soft edges is laid over the head of the base, under the gi layers. It holds only the pixels in which that render differs from the base render. The Standard base shows the published head: the fringe, black hair, clean-shaven. Dean's base shows his coil buzz cut with black hair and stubble.

The pants and belt layers are the published ones for every head, and all layers are added on top exactly as before, so jacket, pants and belt colors work the same for every head.

**Where the chosen head shows.** It shows in every portrait and figure of the player's Unreal athlete. One component draws them all (`UnrealAthlete` in `src/components/UnrealAthlete.jsx`), so they follow a choice together:

- The top bar shows it in the round portrait beside the fighter's name. Pages narrower than 360 pixels hide that portrait.
- The Overview shows it in the round portraits of the roster, one for each saved fighter with its own head, and on the athlete in the hero where the hero has room for one.
- The Profile shows it on the stage and, on pages up to 760 pixels wide, on the two small copies in the Fighter Creator: the head beside Hair Color and Beard, and the figure beside the gi swatches.
- Training shows it on the figure beside the proficiency test.
- Insights shows it on the rank path.
- The Arena shows it in the round portrait of the player's card in the scoreboard.

The portrait in the Arena scoreboard came with the head choices. `AthleteScore` in `src/components/CombatArenaView.jsx` draws the two cards of the scoreboard (`.arena-top`). An athlete with the Unreal look gets a round portrait there (`span.mini-portrait` around an `UnrealAthlete`): the chosen face, hair and beard above the collar of the jacket in its color. Only the player's fighter can have that look, so every opponent keeps the two gi swatches (`span.snapshot-kit`), and so does a player with the classic look. The name beside the picture says who it is, so the portrait is hidden from screen readers, as the swatches are. Its styles are at the end of `src/game/arena.css`: a gold ring and a size of 3.5rem, 3rem on pages up to 1120 pixels wide and 2.5rem where the scoreboard is narrow. The scoreboard measures itself (a container query, `jiuquest-versus`): it is narrow when it has 560 pixels or less inside. That is so on phones, and again in wider windows where the Arena has two columns (from 761 pixels, and from 981 pixels beside the technique list). There the picture also stands above the name on both sides of the scoreboard (`:has(.fighter-card.has-portrait)`), so each name keeps the whole width of its side and a long word in a name does not break inside the word. Older rules in `src/styles.css` hide `.fighter-card .mini-portrait` at some widths. The rule in `arena.css` outranks them, so the portrait shows at every width.

The chosen head also shows on the player's athlete in the Unreal standing position still (Training's position reference for standing techniques, the Arena when an exchange settles on the feet, the Overview's scenario teaser), whichever of the two places the player is in. The other athlete in that still is the opponent and is always the published athlete.

**Where it does not.** A pre-rendered clip is one video of the two published athletes, so the Arena's stance loops, match moments and technique clips show them whatever head the player chose, exactly as they show a white and a navy gi whatever the player wears; the legend under every clip says "Athletes and colors are fixed in this clip." The narrow Overview hero shows the poster of the standing-stance clip, so it shows the published athletes too. The seven stance pictures of the stance pickers are illustrations of the published athlete. The Unity animations and the classic position pictures are other art. They cannot show the Unreal athlete, so they draw a stand-in look.

**The stand-in look.** The classic position pictures (`src/arena/appearance.js`) and the Unity scene (`src/unity/bridge.js`) draw an athlete from the classic appearance fields: skin tone, hairstyle and hair color, and in the classic pictures the gender and the patch emblem too. A player with the Unreal athlete cannot see or change those fields in the Fighter Creator. So in those pictures the fields are taken from the chosen head: `classicStandIn(fighter)` in `src/unreal/athleteStandIn.js` returns the fighter with the nearest classic value for the face, the hairstyle and the hair color, and without a patch.

| Head choice | Classic field | Value drawn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face | Gender and skin tone | Standard: `boy`, `#d99b71`. Dean: `boy`, `#a86b4d`. |
| Hair | Hairstyle | Side-swept fringe: `wave cut`. Buzz cut, skin fade: `crew cut`. Short coily, low fade: `short fade`. Coil buzz cut: `crew cut`. Shaved: `bald`. The classic list has two short cuts for the three here, so the two buzz cuts share one. |
| Hair color | Hair color | Black: `#11131c`. Brown: `#3b251b`. Blond: `#d7c09a`. Grey: `#6b6f86`. |

The skin tones and hair colors are swatches of the classic palette, and the hairstyles are names from the classic list (`src/storage.js`). Nothing is saved: the stand-in is a copy made for the picture. The fighter keeps its own classic fields and gets them back by choosing the Classic look again. The aura is chosen in the Fighter Creator with either look, so a classic picture draws the one saved with the fighter. A fighter with the Classic look and every opponent are drawn as they are.

Three components use the stand-in. `GrapplingSnapshotStage` uses it for the classic position picture; the copy keeps the head fields and the gi colors, so the Unreal standing still reads the same fighter. `UnityGrapplingStage` and `UnrealGrapplingStage` use it for the scene they send (`toUnityAthlete(classicStandIn(fighter), ...)`): the Unity scene, and the local live preview, which takes the same appearance fields. Where a classic picture shows the exact position, its caption tells such a player what it is: "Classic art · your gi colors, hair color and a similar haircut". The picture's description for screen readers says the same: "Classic art. Each athlete wears their own gi and belt colors. Your athlete has your hair color and a similar haircut." A player with the classic look reads "Position snapshot · your appearance" there, and the description names the appearance choices of that look.

**Files.** `node tools/build_unreal_athlete_heads.mjs <heads.json> [--public <folder>] [--module <file>] [--debug <folder>]` writes the pictures and the two modules beside them. `--public` names another folder than `public` to write the pictures under. `--module` names another data module; its name must end in `.data.js`, and the pins are written beside it as `.pins.js`. A trial run names both: the tool stops when `--public` is given without `--module`, because pictures written to another folder must not be described by the page's own modules. `--debug` names a folder for PNG copies of the bases, jacket layers and head pictures, for looking at. The tool needs sharp, which it takes from wherever Node resolves it or from pnpm's store in `node_modules`.

The tool publishes 232 files, about 2.2 MB in all: 84 for the front view and 148 for the standing still.

| Under | Files | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `public/assets/unreal-athlete/athlete-01/heads` (84 files) | `base-dean.webp`, `jacket-dean.webp` | Dean's whole 900 by 1760 base picture and his jacket layer. |
| | 72 `<face>-<hair>-<color>-<beard>.webp` | A 324 by 306 picture with transparency, laid at 261, 0 of the 900 by 1760 picture. |
| | 10 `thumb-<face>-<hair>.webp` | 128 by 128 heads for the buttons: each face with each hairstyle, with black hair and no beard. |
| `public/assets/unreal-positions/standing/heads` (148 files) | `lead-base-dean.webp`, `other-base-dean.webp` | The whole 1620 by 1080 still with Dean in one place and the published athlete in the other. |
| | `lead-jacket-dean.webp`, `other-jacket-dean.webp` | The jacket layer of the athlete in that place when he is Dean, written at the WebP quality of the published standing jacket layers (96; see Unreal position stills below). |
| | 72 `lead-...` and 72 `other-...` | A 250 by 280 picture with transparency for the head of the athlete in that place, laid at 500, 80 (lead) or 880, 40 (other) of the 1620 by 1080 still. |

Each 72 is the 74 heads less the two that the base pictures already show. Outside the Fighter Creator only the files of the chosen head are downloaded: for a default fighter none of them, otherwise at most one base, one jacket layer and one small head picture per athlete picture. A head picture is about 12.5 kB on average for the front view and about 7.3 kB for the standing still. The Fighter Creator also loads the pictures on its buttons, at most six different files for one choice, about 1.4 kB each. `src/unreal/athleteHeads.data.js` is the little the page needs (folders, sizes, where a head lies; a picture's name follows from the choice). `src/unreal/athleteHeads.pins.js` holds the size and SHA-256 of every file, the hashes of the renders they were made from, and the SHA-256 of the published layers the tool read (`layers`, see below); it is for the tests and is never loaded by the page.

The renders are not in this repository. `heads.json` names each PNG with its size and SHA-256, and the tool refuses any other file. It stops in each of these cases:

- A render is missing, is not the file `heads.json` describes, or does not have the pixel size of its scene (2400 by 3000 for the front view, 2560 by 1440 for the standing still).
- A choice has no render, a choice has two renders, a render is for a choice the options file does not list, or two choices name the same render (the same file or the same SHA-256). Every choice needs a render of its own, because one render standing in for a second choice would publish a wrong head.
- The tool's own reference render of the published athlete differs from the published base by more than 1.5 of 255 on average (on November 17, 2026 it measured 0.67 for the front view and 0.63 for the standing still). The published gi layers are added to bases made from this renderer's pictures and must fit them. The check runs for the front view and for the standing still. It tells another floor and light apart (renders in the dojo set and in the studio look differ by a mean of about 10), but not other hands: renders with the hands of before November 16, 2026 measured 1.11 against the published base of the ready hands.
- The variant render of the published head does not match the reference render of the same scene: inside the head box, more than 1 percent of the pixels (`SETTINGS.donorLimit`) differ by more than 12 of 255. The head pictures of the Standard face are laid over the published base, so the variants must have been rendered as that base was. A camera or an exposure that drifted between the two runs would lay every head displaced or off-tone. The check runs for the front view and for each of the two places in the standing still.
- A head picture reaches the edge of its box, where it would be cut off, or shows nothing that its face's base does not. One exception: a part of a head picture that would reach the edge of the box, and in which no pixel differs from the base by more than 20 of 255 (`faintEdge` in `HEAD_PICTURE`), is left out of the picture instead. Such a part is light the head throws on what lies beside it, not the head. In the dojo set the beard changes the light on the lead athlete's hand, which reaches into the lower corner of his head box, by up to 17 of 255. So in the 37 lead pictures that add or remove a beard, that part of the hand is left out and keeps the light of the base (the report counts the pictures and the largest difference left out). A part that differs by more still stops the tool. Before November 17, 2026 the tool stopped at such a part, and the studio renders had none. The rule that makes a head picture, this exception included, is `headPicture` in `tools/unreal_athlete_head_picture.mjs` (its numbers are `HEAD_PICTURE` there), which the tool imports and `tests/unrealAthleteHeads.test.js` tries on small made-up boxes: a faint part at the edge that differs by 20 is left out, one that differs by 21 still stops the tool, so does a head that reaches the edge itself, a faint part away from the edge or joined to the head stays in the picture, and a picture that would hold only faint parts at the edge is nothing, which stops the tool too.
- A flag stands without its value, or `--public` is given without `--module`.

Nothing published is touched until every picture is built. The tool writes into a folder beside each published one (`heads.building`), exchanges the two at the end, and only then writes the two modules. A run that stops therefore leaves the published pictures and both modules exactly as they were. Building the same renders again gives the same files, byte for byte.

**The heads belong to the layers they were built on.** A head picture lies on the published base of its picture, and Dean's jacket layers are the published jacket layers with his own collar light, so the head pictures fit only the layers they were built on. The tool reads five published layers, always from the working copy it stands in: the front view's base and jacket layer, and the standing still's base and its two jacket layers. The pins keep the SHA-256 of each as the tool read it (`layers` in `athleteHeads.pins.js`), and `tests/unrealAthleteHeads.test.js` fails, naming the file, when any of the five is no longer that file. Rebuilding the layers of either picture (another floor, light, pose or WebP quality) therefore fails the unit tests until the heads are built again from renders of the same scene as the new layers. Before November 17, 2026 nothing in the site caught that: the test pinned the head pictures but not the layers under them, and the tool's own check runs only when the tool runs (and does not see other hands, see above). The test also requires that the tool reads published layers in that one place only, and that Dean's standing jacket layers have the WebP quality of the published standing jacket layers (96 in `tools/build_unreal_position_layers.mjs`; at 88 a white jacket and white pants meet in a faint dotted line). It was tried on a copy of the working tree on November 17, 2026: with the standing layers built again in the studio look (and their own pins moved with them), with the dojo layers written at quality 88 (the base stays the same file, the jacket layers change), and with the front base encoded again, it failed each time and every other test passed; with the files as committed it passes.

Dean's head was made for a bigger build and keeps a little shoulder skin above the cloth: the two clavicle bones of his head mesh are collapsed for the render, and wherever a base still shows anything but the reference render well inside the cloth of the gi, the tool takes the reference render's pixels (the report prints how many). "Cloth" is where the gi brightens when it turns white and is nearly black in the black-gi reference; skin under the chin brightens too, because the gi lights it, and is left alone. The white-gi render of Dean gets the same pixels from the white-gi reference, so the jacket piece around his head stays the gi's light. In the standing still a small dark notch, about 10 by 12 of its 1620 by 1080 pixels, is left between the collar and the back of his neck where his shoulder skin was taken out. The studio pictures published before had it in the same place; the dojo renders did not change it.

**How the heads were rendered** (the renderer's own project, not this repository). Each head is a built MetaHuman: the athlete's editable character with another hairstyle and the stubble beard and moustache, or Dean's character with the athlete's body. Dean gets the athlete's body from the athlete's body DNA, exported and imported as a whole rig, so the two bodies share one skeleton bone for bone; pinning Dean's body measurements to the athlete's left differences of up to 2.5 cm. For a still, the face mesh, its materials, the grooms with their bindings and, for Dean, the body's skin material are written onto the athlete that stands in the published scene, the beard and hair are shown or hidden, the hair color parameters are written to dynamic material instances, and one Movie Render Queue still with 64 samples is taken; nothing is saved. The front view is Codex's Pack A scene, reproduced first (see the 0.67 above); its head renders were made on November 15 and 16, 2026, and its 84 pictures have not changed since.

The standing still's heads were rendered again on November 17, 2026 in the dojo set (`dojo_tatami`, see The dojo set below), in the scene its layers are built from: frame 0 of Codex's ready-hand sequence of the fighter's side view (`/Game/CodexHands_20261115_v1/LS_WrestlingLoop_fighter_side`), the side camera, 2560 by 1440 and the map's own exposure, with each choice's head on both athletes. Ten engine runs gave 81 stills: the 74 choices, three reference stills of the published athletes (black gi, and each athlete's gi in white), and four of Dean beside the published athlete (in each place, in a black and in a white gi). The reference stills differ from the five passes the standing layers are built from by a mean of 0.022 to 0.026 of 255, with 0.001 percent of the pixels more than 12 apart. Before that day the standing heads were studio renders: the 74 head renders still had the hands of before November 16, 2026, and only Dean's base and jacket pictures had been rendered again with the ready hands (#29).

What was checked on November 17, 2026, by measuring and looking, not by a test: each of the 144 head pictures of the standing still, laid over its face's published base as the page lays it, gives back the render of its choice inside its head box to a mean of at most 5.6 of 255 where the picture is opaque (WebP loss on fine hair), at most 3.0 on its soft edge and at most 1.2 in the rest of the box. No head picture shows in the outer two pixels of its box; the nearest comes within 8 pixels of the edge. Around the heads the boxes hold only the black background (and, in the lead's box, a corner of his hand); the lit tatami begins more than 100 pixels below them. With a white jacket and white pants, Dean's jacket layers meet the pants near the jacket outline within a mean of 2.2 of 255 and at most 12, as the published athlete's layers do. Claude looked at the standing still with seven heads, each in two sets of gi and belt colors, whole and at three times their size around the head: as the lead, Dean with the grey coil buzz cut and stubble, Dean shaved, the Standard face with the blond short coily cut, and the Standard face shaved; as the other athlete, Dean with his own head, Dean with the brown fringe, and the Standard face with the blond skin fade and stubble. Claude also looked at the four hair colors of four hairstyles beside the front view's. No edge of a head box shows, and no head has a light or dark rim. Each hair and beard color reads as its name, with two remarks: under the set's lights the blond and grey look lighter than in the front view, and on the short coily cut black and brown look almost alike, as they do in the front view.

On November 17, 2026 the browser suites of the release gate showed these pictures on the dojo layers. `tools/test_unreal_athlete_heads.mjs` found the head it chooses (Dean with the grey short coily cut and stubble) in the player's place of the standing still, on Dean's own base and jacket layer, in the Overview's scenario teaser, in Training and as the Arena's standing-headlock reference, and the classic picture in place of the still when the head picture of another choice could not load. `tools/test_unreal_position_still.mjs` checked the colors the browser paints on the dojo layers at its seven places, with the published head. A reviewer also looked at screenshots taken in Chromium at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844: the standing still in Training with the published head and six chosen ones (three at the narrow size), in the Overview's teaser with the published head and with Dean's own head, and in the Arena with the blond skin fade, as the standing-headlock reference and after an exchange that settled standing.

**If a picture cannot load.** The athlete never shows part of a head (`useAthletePicture` in `src/components/UnrealAthlete.jsx`). What stands in depends on the file that is missing:

- Only the small head picture is missing: the chosen face shows as its base picture has it. For the Standard face that is the published athlete. For Dean it is Dean with his coil buzz cut, black hair and stubble.
- The base picture of the chosen face is missing, or Dean's own jacket layer, which belongs to his base: the published athlete stands in.
- A gi layer of the published athlete is missing: the other layers show without it, as they did before the heads.

The choice itself is kept and saved in each case. Whichever file failed, the files are tried again twice: 4 seconds after the failed attempt and, if that one fails too, 15 seconds after it. After such a try the athlete's own pictures are made anew in the page, because an image that failed there keeps its broken picture for as long as its address stays the same. After the second try the stand-in stays until the choice changes. A failed download is not remembered, so choosing that head again asks for its files again, and so does the next athlete on the page that needs them. The `data-head` attribute of the athlete names the head that is painted: the chosen one, the base's own when only the head picture is missing, or `published`.

While the files of a new choice are on their way, the picture painted before stays, and the athlete carries the class `is-changing`. It keeps that class while a file that failed is still to be tried again, because the stand-in then shows another head than the pressed buttons. If the wait lasts longer than 0.35 seconds, a line of light runs along the top edge of the picture (`UnrealAthlete.css`). Where only the head is in view (the round portraits and the head preview of the Fighter Creator), the line runs along the top of the square that shows. Under reduced motion the line appears and stands still.

The first time an athlete shows, it waits for more than the downloads: its own base image and head image in the page must have finished loading. A browser cache that has to ask the server again would otherwise let the gi show a moment before the person. Once shown, the athlete stays shown, and a later choice swaps to files that are already loaded. An image that fails in the page counts as finished too, so nothing can hide the athlete for good.

The standing still has no stand-in athlete (`src/components/UnrealPositionStill.jsx`). It is used only when every file it needs has loaded: the base, the gi layers and the picture laid over a chosen head. If any of them is missing, the stage shows the classic picture, with the stand-in look, and never a half-colored athlete or another face. No timer tries again there. A failed load is not remembered, so the next stage that needs the still asks for its files again.

**Limits.** There are two faces because the purchased assets hold two that can be built without Epic's cloud: the first athlete and Dean, who ships with his textures. The presets that come with the engine need a cloud texture download before they can be built. A second face on both athletes of the standing still has no base picture: with two such faces the lead's would be shown and the other athlete would keep the published head; the game never asks for that today, because opponents have no head choices. Hair color is a small set of rendered colors, not a free color. Clips and stance pictures do not follow the choices (above). The stand-in look is the nearest of a few classic values, not a likeness, and it has no beard.

`tests/unrealAthleteHeads.test.js` pins the choices, the published files and the selection rules, checks that the head pictures belong to the published layers they were built on (above), tries the rule that makes a head picture on small made-up boxes (above), and covers saving and loading, the stand-in look, the scoreboard portrait and the rules for a picture that cannot load. `tools/test_unreal_athlete_heads.mjs`, one of the `test:browser` suites, checks the built site in a browser: the Fighter Creator's buttons, each choice painting its own picture, gi colors with another head chosen, saving and reload, the head in the other places it shows (the Arena's scoreboard portrait and the standing still among them), the classic position picture following the chosen head, pictures that cannot load, and phone widths. It covers still pictures composited by the browser, not Unreal running in the browser.

### Technique clips

Every curriculum technique can play pre-rendered Unreal clips: all 49 in `allMoves` (`src/data/curriculum.js`), including the white-belt starters and the techniques Unity has no animation of. They switch over one technique at a time, as each technique's clips are accepted. A technique's clips go by its clip id, `unrealClipMoveId` in `src/unreal/techniqueIds.js`: a technique Unity animates keeps the id Unity gave it (`double_leg`, `rear_naked_choke`, `trap_roll_escape` and `triangle_choke`), and every other technique is its curriculum id with each `-` written as `_` (`americana-armlock` is `americana_armlock`, `headlock-escape-2` is `headlock_escape_2`). The clip id is the folder a technique's files are rendered into and published under (`<clip id>/<performer>-<camera>.mp4`) and the `moveId` its clips carry in the clip list. `chooseTechniquePicture` in `src/unreal/techniquePicture.js` decides by the clip id, and `GrapplingStage` follows it in Training (the player performs) and in the Arena (the winner of the exchange performs; `resolveArenaClipPlayback` gives the clip id and the performer):

- A technique plays its Unreal clips only once every performer the game can show has a published clip of it: both `fighter` and `opponent`, or the `fighter` alone for a technique the rival never performs from its clip start (`techniqueClipPerformers` and `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS` in `src/unreal/techniquePicture.js`, which `techniqueClipReady` follows). Training plays only the `fighter` clip, and the Arena plays an `opponent` clip only after the rival wins with the technique in an exchange that started at its clip start. The rival never does that with 15 techniques: the nine previews (`purple_omoplata`, `purple_darce`, `purple_x_guard_sweep`, `brown_pressure_pass`, `brown_knee_cut`, `brown_crucifix`, `black_berimbolo`, `black_leg_drag` and `black_bow_arrow`), because `chooseNpcMove` in `src/App.jsx` never gives the rival a preview technique above white belt, and six whose clip start the rival is never in, because no start and no technique ever puts the player on top there (`double_ankle_sweep`, `blocking_standing_punch_from_ground` and `hook_sweep` from `open-guard-bottom`; `headlock_scissors_choke` and `headlock_escape_2` from `headlock`; `standing_headlock_defense` from `standing-headlock`). Each of those switches over with its fighter clips alone; a rival's win with one, which the game never produces, would still show what it shows today, because a clip plays only for a performer with a view of their own. Every other technique needs clips of both performers. Until a technique switches over it plays exactly what it played before: for the four Unity animates, Unity on the release build and the position picture elsewhere; for every other technique, the position picture, headed "Position reference" in Training. Once a technique's clips play, its heading in Training is "Movement preview". A technique is never half Unreal and half Unity, with one exception the owner decided on: the double leg, whose unfinished fighter clip plays for the player while a rival's double leg keeps the Unity animation (see Owner-published double-leg draft at the end).
- `tests/unrealRivalReach.test.js` keeps that list honest by working it out again from the game's own rules. It walks every Arena state the game can reach from every start the Arena offers (Standing Start, Kneeling Start, each Position Start option and the profile's start poses). In each state the player may choose any curriculum technique offered from the player's side, and may win only where the counter rule lets it; the rival answers with any technique `chooseNpcMove` can return, for every rival belt and including its Protect Your Position fallback (the function is cut from `src/App.jsx` and run as written, and pinned line for line, so a change to the rival's rule fails the test); either side may win, and `resolveArenaOutcome` (`src/arena/outcome.js`) gives the next state. The rival performs a technique from its clip start when some state the game reaches puts the rival there with the technique offered to it. The test fails, naming the ids to add and remove, when `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS` is not exactly the techniques the rival never performs from their clip start, in curriculum order. It also asserts the converse: every technique's fighter clip can be seen, in Training (which lists 45 techniques) or, for the four white-belt starters Training does not list, after the player's Arena win from Standing. The search ignores scores and the round limit, so it allows more than the game does, and a clip it never asks for is never shown.
- A technique's clips are rendered from one position, its clip start: the first position it starts in (`move.starts[0]`, `unrealClipStart` in `src/unreal/techniqueIds.js`). Training shows every technique it lists in that position; it lists the blue to black belt techniques, so the clips of the four white-belt starters (`protect_position`, `basic_punch`, `basic_kick` and `basic_tackle`) play only in the Arena. In the Arena its clip plays only after an exchange that started there: the exchange result records where the exchange started, seen from the winner's side and in the position ids the game offers techniques by (`startedFrom`, `src/App.jsx`), and `resolveArenaClipPlayback` asks for the clip only when that is the winning technique's clip start. This matters for the eight techniques that start in more than one position, each rendered from the first: `protect_position` (all 26 positions, `standing` first; it is also the rival's fallback move), `basic_punch` and `basic_tackle` (`standing`, then `kneeling`), `guillotine_choke` (`standing`, then `clinch`), `standing_headlock_defense` (`standing-headlock`, then `standing`), `guillotine_defense` (`clinch`, then `standing`), `purple_darce` (`side-top`, then `scramble`) and `brown_crucifix` (`back-control`, then `scramble`). `node tools/import_unreal_clips.mjs <Unreal-render-pack/clips> <ffmpeg bin dir>` copies each MP4 from the render pack unchanged, after checking it against the pack's `technique-clips.json` (size and SHA-256) and with ffprobe (H.264 High, yuv420p, picture size, frame rate, frame count, index before media, no audio). It writes a smaller lossy WebP poster beside each one with ffmpeg's libwebp (quality 82): from the pack's lossless poster for a looping clip, and from frame 0 of the video for a clip that plays once (`"posterFrame": 0`). It writes `src/unreal/techniqueClips.data.js`, which pins every published file. A technique clip, one whose move is the clip id of any curriculum technique (`TECHNIQUE_IDS`, all 49), must have `"loops": false` and `"draft": false`, the same frame count in every view of one performer, and an acceptance line in `tools/unreal_clip_acceptance.json` (`{ "file", "sha256", "acceptedOn" }`) for that exact file; without one it is left out and listed under `notAccepted`. That rule is the same for every technique, and no technique clip has an acceptance line yet (the list holds one line, for the match greeting; see Match moments below): the owner-published double-leg draft is not in it, the importer leaves that take out like any other draft, and a separate tool publishes it (see Owner-published double-leg draft at the end). The importer writes the whole list anew and deletes no file, so an import in place drops that draft's row and leaves its two files: it says so on its error output, the unit tests fail (`tests/unrealOwnerDraft.test.js`, `tests/unrealTechniqueClips.test.js` and the draft's test in `tests/techniqueSheet.test.js`), and `node tools/publish_unreal_clip_draft.mjs --restore` puts the row back. Stance clips need no acceptance line. A match moment clip (`match_greeting`, `match_win` or `match_loss`) is taken in like a technique clip, with rules of its own; see Match moments below. A move name is lower-case letters, digits and underscores, starting with a letter, and a move that is neither a technique's clip id, a stance clip nor a match moment is refused. `--out <dir>` writes everything under another folder instead, for a dry run. The importer is Node because the machine that imports has no Python; it replaced `tools/import_unreal_clips.py`, and re-running it on the November 13, 2026 pack publishes the same files byte for byte. `tests/unrealTechniqueClips.test.js` fails if a file is replaced, if a clip names neither a curriculum technique's clip id, a stance clip nor a match moment, or if a file is published without being pinned.

The outfits in a clip are fixed: the player's fighter in a white gi and the opponent in navy, both in black belts, whatever the player's own fighter wears. So are the heads: a clip shows the published athlete whatever face, hair and beard the player chose in the Fighter Creator. The stage says so beside the picture ("Athletes and colors are fixed in this clip."). Both athletes are the same character. Codex's pack manifest records for each of its takes that its author played the encoded file at normal and quarter speed. Twelve of the 16 loops published now are the dojo set's renders of those takes, which were checked by machine and by looking at stills, not played at both speeds (see The dojo set above). The other four, the side and front views of the boxer shell's and the Muay Thai guard's loops, are not Codex's takes: they are Claude's motion-captured loops, every frame of which was looked at by the operator who rendered them and by two reviewers (see The boxer shell's loop and The Muay Thai guard's loop above). No qualified choreography review has been done of any clip (`choreographyReviewed` is false).

`tools/test_unreal_clip_stage.mjs` checks the built site in a browser: the stance clip plays, loops, pauses and changes camera; reduced motion starts paused on the poster; a clip that cannot load gives way to the position snapshot; the stance gives way once an exchange settles; 390 and 320 pixel layouts. It also walks every technique: one with published clips of every performer it needs (both, or the fighter alone for a technique in `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS`) is checked in Training and in a player's Arena win against the real files. A technique with the exact owner-published draft (today the double leg) is checked apart: Training and the player's Arena win play the draft, with no Front view and its poster not compared with frame 0, and a rival's win must show the Unity stage. Every other one must show what it showed before on the release default, the Unity stage for the other three techniques Unity animates and the position picture for the rest, in Training, or in the Arena for the white-belt starters Training does not list. It covers a video file played by the browser. It is not evidence of Unreal rendering in the browser or of a stream. The local live preview (`?engine=unreal` on localhost) is unchanged and renders the stance itself.

### Match moments

Three more pre-rendered clips belong to a match rather than to a technique or a stance. One of them is published: the greeting, from the side (see the source take notes below). The win and the loss are not, so a finished match shows exactly what it showed before them. `src/unreal/matchMomentIds.js` names them (`MATCH_MOMENT_IDS`); it has no imports, so the importer can read the names without loading the clip list it writes. `src/unreal/matchMoments.js` hands those names on and finds a moment's clips (`findMatchMoment`).

Claude rendered it that day in Unreal Engine 5.8.4, in its own render project and in the dojo set (see The dojo set above), with the camera, exposure, colours and jackets of `standing_stance/fighter-side`. The clip starts on frame 27 of `standing_stance/fighter-side`, the frame that loop's poster shows, which is the still behind the greeting while its files arrive; the athletes rise and step in, bump fists, step back into the crouch, and the clip ends on that loop's frame 0, where the loop that follows starts. The encoder compared both ends with the published loop's own render of those frames: the recorded camera, places of both athletes and poses are equal, and the pictures differ by a mean of 0.0070 and 0.0072 of 255 levels. The MP4 is H.264 High, `yuv420p`, with one key frame (frame 0) and the index before the data; every decoded frame is within a mean of 0.72 of 255 levels of the frame it was encoded from. At about 121 kB a second it is 1 percent over the rate the moment encoder aims for, 120 kB a second (the side views of the held-pose stance loops use 107 to 114 kB a second and their front views 121 to 136 kB; the motion-captured loops of the boxer shell and the Muay Thai guard use 126 to 168 kB). Only Claude has looked at it: the operator who rendered it at every frame of the full-speed preview and of the encoded MP4 (at 480 by 270, and frames 33 to 38, around the bump, at full size), and the site engineer at frames of it in the built site. The owner has not seen it yet, and there is no choreography review (`choreographyReviewed` is false). The render's notes name its visible flaws: the fists touch for two frames, a quick tap; both athletes play the same take, so they move as mirror images, and the white athlete stands turned a little away from the camera; the fists meet at about chin height. In the built site the white athlete, the player's own, has his back half to the camera at the bump, so his face does not show there. A reviewer who judged the frames as a coach would found it a respectful fist bump, but a formal and stiff one: the fists are held up at face level for about half a second before the bump and after it, the two athletes move in lockstep down to the setting of the lead foot, and a jiu-jitsu slap-and-bump is usually lower, at chest height, and quicker. None of this held the release back; For the renderer below says what the next take should change. There is no front view, so a player who looks at the stance loop from the front gets no greeting (see The greeting below). The motion-capture pack is the owner's. It is not in this repository and must not be added to it: only the rendered clip and its poster are published. Its licence is marked "no AI use", so neither the pack nor clips made from it go to a generative tool.

Why the win and the loss are not published. Quiet versions of both were rendered on November 17, 2026: both athletes standing, one of them nodding, because the purchased packs hold no celebration or tired take that meets the bar. Files. A moment is rendered once, with the player's athlete as the `fighter`: `<id>/fighter-side.mp4` with its poster `<id>/fighter-side.webp`, and, if wanted, `<id>/fighter-front.mp4` with `<id>/fighter-front.webp`. There is no `opponent` take. A moment needs its side view: `findMatchMoment` returns nothing for a moment whose side view is not published, whichever view is asked for, so a front view on its own can be imported but is shown to nobody. With the side view published it returns the view asked for when that view is published too, and the side view otherwise. The outfits are the fixed ones of every clip, the player in white and the rival in navy, and the stage shows the same colour legend.

Importer. `tools/import_unreal_clips.mjs` takes a match moment as a third kind of clip beside stances and techniques, along the technique's path. An entry whose `moveId` is one of the three ids must have `"loops": false`, `"draft": false`, `"performerSide": "fighter"`, `"camera"` `side` or `front`, a picture of 1280 by 720 at 30 frames a second, and between 45 and 240 frames, a second and a half to eight seconds (`MATCH_MOMENT_CLIP`). Both views of one moment must have the same frame count. Everything the importer asks of any clip still holds: the manifest's size and SHA-256, H.264 High, `yuv420p`, no sound, the index before the media, and a same-name poster with the video's pixel size in the pack. The poster the site publishes is cut from frame 0 of the video (`"posterFrame": 0`), as for every clip that plays once. An entry that breaks a rule stops the whole import. An entry marked `"draft": true` is left out and listed under `draftsLeftOut`, as before. A name that is none of the three kinds, `match_draw` for example, still stops the import, and the message names the three moments. The importer reads the three ids from `src/unreal/matchMomentIds.js` and loads no module that reads `src/unreal/techniqueClips.data.js`, the clip list it writes: it still starts, and writes that file again, when the file is missing or broken (after a merge, say).

Acceptance. A moment waits for a line in `tools/unreal_clip_acceptance.json` exactly as a technique clip does, one line for each file: `{ "file": "match_greeting/fighter-side.mp4", "sha256": "<the SHA-256 of that file>", "acceptedOn": "YYYY-MM-DD" }`. Without its line a file is left out and listed under `notAccepted`, and the import goes on. A new take has a new hash and needs a new line. A moment has no other switch: once its side view is imported, the Arena plays it.

The greeting. `startCombat` in `src/App.jsx`, where Standing Start, Restart match and the Overview's Open Arena all pass, sets `combat.greeting` when the match starts standing. The flag is part of the match state, which is never saved. The mat window shows the greeting while the flag is set, both athletes are on their feet with nothing settled, a `match_greeting` clip is published, motion is allowed, and the stance loop on screen is the one the greeting was rendered for: the standard loop (`standing_stance`, the player's athlete's) in a view the greeting has, today the side view (`endsOnLoop` in `GrapplingStage`). The greeting rises out of that loop and settles back into its first frame, so there it runs in and out without a seam. It shows in the stance loop's picture box, under the heading "Match greeting", plays once from its first frame, silent and inline, and its one control is Skip. It opens on the view the stance loop is showing (`loopView` in `GrapplingStage`), so the camera does not flip between the loop, the greeting and the loop. Until its own first frame has arrived, its box shows the still of the stance loop it took the place of (that clip's poster, in the view the loop is showing), so the box is not black while the files load. When it ends, the stance loop takes over in the same box, as it did before. A greeting that plays to its end hands its last frame over, which is the loop's first frame, so the loop takes over from the picture the greeting stopped on. At its end the greeting draws its last frame into a canvas (`onLastFrame` in `MomentClip`, `src/components/UnrealClipStage.jsx`), and `GrapplingStage` hands it to the stance loop that mounts in its place (`opening`, for that one render). The loop lays it over its video (`.unreal-clip-handover`, trimmed as the video is) until the video has presented a frame of its own while it plays (`requestVideoFrameCallback`; where a browser has none, the first time the video's clock moves), so the loop's poster, a frame halfway through the loop, never shows between the greeting's last frame and the loop's frame 0, also when the loop's file is slow. The video itself, its poster included, is the one the loop always has. A loop the player paused rests on that frame, the one it starts from, until Play is pressed; choosing the other view, or another stance, drops it for good. A greeting that is skipped, cut short or gives way hands nothing over: the loop then opens on its own poster, a cut, as before. The greeting waits only so long for its picture (`PASSING_MOMENT_PATIENCE` in `src/unreal/clipPlayback.js`): two seconds for the first moving frame, and one second for the picture to move again once it has run out of data. When a wait is up the greeting is over and the stance loop takes over, so a download that is slow or hangs without failing never leaves the box on a still, and the greeting never plays late. The greeting never holds the match up: keyboard focus goes to the technique heading as after any start, and a technique can be chosen at once. The same goes for Restart match while a technique is being chosen. That restart does not change the phase, which the usual focus rule after a start goes by, and a browser that does not focus a button it clicks (Safari) leaves focus where it was: on the stance loop's own Pause or camera button, it would go with the loop when the greeting takes its place. So Restart match notes whether focus is there, and once the greeting is on screen, focus that fell to the page goes to the technique heading (`stanceFocusRef` in `src/components/CombatArenaView.jsx`). Focus anywhere else stays where it is. The flag is cleared when the greeting ends, is skipped or cannot play, when a technique is chosen, when the starting stance is changed, when an exchange settles or the opponent changes, and when the player leaves the Arena, so coming back to a running match does not greet again. There is no greeting for someone who asks for reduced motion (its files are not fetched), under the local live preview (`?engine=unreal`), where the stance loop is not published, or over any loop other than the one it was rendered for: the loop of the player's own named stance (karate, say), the loop of a rival whose stance has one of its own (sumo base and sambo crouch; Luana Rocha, the second default rival, holds sumo base), and the standard loop seen from the front, since the greeting has no front view. Those loops carry on as they did before the greeting was published, and its files are not fetched. Greetings rendered for those loops and views can bring them in later (see For the renderer below). A stance loop the player paused is greeted like any other (a pause on the loop is not a request for reduced motion, and the greeting has its own Skip), and is still paused when it comes back: the mat window keeps the loop's chosen view, and whether the player paused it, for as long as the loop or the greeting in its place is the picture (`stanceCamera` and `stancePlays` in `GrapplingStage`), and hands both to the loop that follows the greeting. Any other picture forgets both, as before.

The end of a match. A finished match (`combat.result.finished`) ends on `match_win` or `match_loss` by who won the match (`result.matchPlayerWins`), by submission or by decision. That is what a pixel that is part gi and part belt shows when both are white. An earlier cap at the brighter of the two white renders also removed the bright line, but on a pixel the belt's edge half covers, each white render is only half bright, so that cap left a one-pixel dark line along a light belt on a light gi; the linear-light cap leaves neither. With every color white, the edge pixels match the linear-light estimate of an all-white render to a median of under one level; isolated pixels differ by up to about 25, mostly WebP error on the belt layer's sharp edge, and where the render itself shades the belt's edge a one-pixel dip remains. A dark belt on a light gi comes out up to about one pixel crisper at its edges than Codex's render, because the trim takes the gi's share out of a half-covered edge pixel and a dark belt adds little back; at the sizes the site shows the picture that is under half a display pixel.

Each gi and belt layer keeps to its own cloth (`cloth` in the tool's `POSITIONS`). A white gi lights what lies beside it: in its white pass the wearer's neck under the collar, the wrist beside the sleeve and the floor around the feet come out brighter than in the base render. Counted as gi wherever its white pass brightened a pixel by more than 14 levels in every channel, as the tool did before, each jacket layer held patches of its wearer's neck and wrist, and a colored jacket tinted them; the studio layers published before the dojo set put up to 36 levels of a white gi's light on skin more than five pixels from any cloth, and the dojo renders without the rule up to 37. With the rule a pixel is gi or belt only where, in every channel and in linear light, its white pass is more than 4 times as bright as the base render or brighter by more than 0.15 of full light. When the rule was set, on the first dojo renders of November 15, 2026, skin lit by the white gi stayed under about 3 times and 0.08, and cloth in shade was 10 times as bright or more. Measured on the dojo layers published now, in five sets of colors, skin more than five pixels from the cloth takes at most 4 levels from any color and the floor at most 2. Nearer the cloth a soft rim remains, the largest with a white gi: three to five pixels from it up to about 18 levels on skin and 11 on the floor, and one or two pixels from it, in the soft edge every layer has (its region is grown by two pixels and blurred), up to about 39 on skin and 23 on the floor, as on the studio layers (39 and 27).

Unreal colors a whole gi at once, so the tool decides where the jacket ends and the pants begin. For an upright athlete it finds the hem by itself: inside a window, column by column, the darkest row under the skirt (`hem` in the tool's `POSITIONS`). The crouched pose defeats that. Each jacket hangs over a bent hip, the corner of the skirt hangs free beside the hip, and pants show between that corner and the belt, so some columns read jacket, pants, jacket, pants from top to bottom and no single hem row separates them. For this still each athlete's jacket is instead an outline traced by hand in render pixels (`jacket` in `POSITIONS`). It follows the shadow line under the hem, the edges of the skirt corner and the front edge of the skirt up to the belt; everywhere else it runs through background or through the middle of the belt. On the other athlete the dark line up the free corner of the skirt is an edge over the pants only in its lower part: it fades as it climbs and runs on into the jacket, so it is a fold. The outline leaves it where it is still dark and runs straight up into the rolled front edge of the skirt, so the cloth above the fold is jacket (an earlier outline cut straight across that cloth and left a notch of pants under a ledge of jacket). Gi pixels inside the outline are jacket and the rest are pants, blended over four pixels, the same width as the hem blend. The tool's `--debug` pictures paint the jacket blue and the pants red; both were looked at before these layers were published.

Jacket and pants are separate lossy files that meet in that four-pixel ramp, and in a gi of one color their two errors can show as a faint dotted line where the ramp crosses evenly lit cloth. In the dojo light the front edge of the other athlete's skirt is no longer a shaded crease, so the outline crosses evenly lit cloth there, and at the tool's usual WebP quality of 88 a white gi showed that line. The six tinted layers of this still are therefore written at quality 96 (`quality` in `POSITIONS`; the base stays at 90). With jacket and pants both white, base plus jacket plus pants now differs from the white-gi render by a mean of about 2 levels in each channel within four pixels of the outline and by at most 12, where quality 88 left about 3 and up to 17.

The tool exists twice, with the same arithmetic and the same `POSITIONS`: `tools/build_unreal_position_layers.mjs` (Node, with sharp for WebP) and `tools/build_unreal_position_layers.py` (Pillow, NumPy, SciPy). The `.mjs` is the tool of record on a machine without Python, and it built the layers published now: `node tools/build_unreal_position_layers.mjs <Unreal-render-pack/positions> --debug <folder>`. Change both files together. Before the Node version was trusted, it was run on the five renders of the upright still with the settings for that still, and it reproduced all seven published WebP files and the data module byte for byte. The outline method and the belt overlap trim were then added to both, with the same numbers, and later the cloth rule and the quality of a position, both optional; the Python version of those additions has not been run. Without the two optional settings the `.mjs` still rebuilds the seven studio layers and the data module published before the dojo set from Codex's passes byte for byte (checked again on November 17, 2026). The bytes of a WebP file depend on the libwebp release, so the two are not expected to write identical files.

Renderer messages describe playback only; a completed animation must never call `updateProgress`, resolve another Arena turn, or write saved state.

## Epic frontend boundary

The injected interface matches Epic's [UE5.8 PixelStreaming frontend implementation](https://github.com/EpicGamesExt/PixelStreamingInfrastructure/blob/UE5.8/Frontend/library/src/PixelStreaming/PixelStreaming.ts), inspected on 2026-11-08:

- `emitUIInteraction(descriptor)` accepts an object and serializes it; `false` means it could not send. The adapter passes an object, not an already serialized string.
- `addResponseEventListener(name, listener)` receives Unreal response strings; `removeResponseEventListener(name)` removes that listener.
- `addEventListener` and `removeEventListener` register lifecycle callbacks. The adapter invalidates its session on `webRtcDisconnected`, `webRtcFailed`, `streamPreDisconnect`, or `streamReconnect`.

No `emitCommand`, console command or arbitrary URL is exposed. The local frontend uses infrastructure commit `4afd64170216002070a42fa52a87a44d4e97e77c`, matching UE5.8, checked by the loopback launcher. Package and interface checks do not establish that imported assets render correctly.

The caller owns the Epic client, connection, video element and teardown. Create the bridge with a fresh unique ID for each mounted surface (for example `crypto.randomUUID()`). After the client's data channel can send, call `attach(client)`. A send rejection immediately returns the bridge to offline. Retry by attaching after the actual connection is usable, rather than treating a socket connection as success.

```js
const bridge = createUnrealBridge({
  instanceId: crypto.randomUUID(),
  onStatus: updatePlaybackConnectionState,
  onEvent: updatePlaybackOnly,
});
bridge.setScene({
  moveId: 'double_leg',
  performerSide: 'opponent',
  fighter, fighterBelt,
  opponent, opponentBelt,
  speed: 1, camera: 'side', compactViewport: false,
}, false);
// After a real Epic client has a usable data channel:
bridge.attach(client);
// On unmount: bridge.dispose(), then the owner tears down its Epic client.
```

This illustrates the bridge used by `UnrealGrapplingStage`. Its callbacks only update connection/playback UI. The iframe reports an open data channel only after Epic's actual `isDataChannelOpen()` returns true. After each `play`, it waits for a matching receiver `state` acknowledgement and two subsequent `requestVideoFrameCallback` presentations with nonzero video dimensions. The parent accepts that proof only for its current play request. A 45-second initial deadline prevents an endless startup state. `receiverReady` alone does not enable the stage controls or prove visible video, correct assets, quality or performance.

## Receiver protocol, version 1

Every command and response uses this envelope:

```json
{
  "channel": "jiuquest.combat",
  "protocolVersion": 1,
  "instanceId": "unique-mounted-surface",
  "sessionId": "unique-mounted-surface:1",
  "requestId": "unique-mounted-surface:1:setup",
  "type": "configure"
}
```

`attach` advances the session. Only `configure` is sent before the receiver handshake. The receiver must echo the instance, session and setup request and advertise only assets actually imported and ready in that runtime:

```json
{
  "channel": "jiuquest.combat",
  "protocolVersion": 1,
  "instanceId": "unique-mounted-surface",
  "sessionId": "unique-mounted-surface:1",
  "requestId": "unique-mounted-surface:1:setup",
  "type": "ready",
  "capabilities": { "moves": ["standing_stance", "double_leg"] }
}
```

There are 16 recognized canonical IDs in `UNREAL_MOVE_IDS`; recognition does not assert Unreal support. Only advertised IDs can play. The existing four curriculum mappings remain exact: `trap-roll-escape`, `double-leg-takedown`, `triangle-choke`, and `rear-naked-choke`. A generic armbar or pass must not stand in for an unmatched lesson. An empty capability list is valid but enables no playback controls.

The latest queued scene plays after a valid handshake. It contains:

| Field | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `moveId` | Recognized and advertised canonical technique |
| `performerSide` | `fighter` or `opponent`; maps actor identity, not match result |
| `fighter`, `opponent` | Name, skin/hair colors, hair style, gi top/pants colors, belt color, stripes 0–4 only |
| `progress` | Finite number 0–1, shared by both actors |
| `paused` | Boolean |
| `speed` | 0.25, 0.5 or 1 |
| `camera` | `front`, `focus`, `side`, `overhead` or `orbit` |
| `compactViewport` | Boolean composition hint |

For a player who chose the Unreal athlete, the skin tone, hair color and hair style in `fighter` are the stand-in look of the chosen head (see Faces, hair and beards above). The scene carries no face, beard or head id.

After readiness, the controls are `pause`, `resume`, `seek`, `reset`, `setSpeed`, `setCamera`, `setViewport`, and `requestState`. Controls carry the full sanitized scene snapshot and a new request ID; only `play` should replace/load a clip. Seek and reset pause; reset seeks to zero. Appearance changes use `setScene`, which sends a new `play` snapshot. No separate appearance or actor-clock command exists. A known but unadvertised selection pauses the previous scene and disables controls until a supported selection is made.

The receiver sends `state` and `completed` with the exact current request ID, current move ID, progress and playing boolean. A completion must report progress 1 and playing false. `error` has a plain message of 1–240 characters and optionally the active move ID; a configure error uses the setup request. Errors take the bridge offline. Stale sessions, stale requests, wrong moves and protocol versions are ignored. The receiver must similarly discard superseded asynchronous loads and commands from prior sessions.

All responses are JSON strings limited to 8,192 UTF-8 bytes. IDs are bounded and contain only letters, digits, colon, underscore and hyphen. Outbound values are explicitly selected; extra renderer fields cannot propagate scores or persistence actions to the app. The Unreal receiver must independently validate the same schema and restrict operations to this command list. These bounds are not session authentication: the hosting layer must authorize and isolate clients.

## Disconnect and lifecycle behavior

- A missing application handshake times out after 15 seconds. Transport connectivity never enables controls by itself.
- Visibility/reduced-motion pauses and viewport hints received before readiness update the queued scene without sending a control early or resetting saved playback progress.
- Stream loss, rejected sends and receiver errors disable controls and freeze the last confirmed progress with playing false.
- Reattach changes the session, repeats the handshake and sends the latest scene paused at its last confirmed position. It does not resolve another turn or award a rep.
- Dispose removes response/lifecycle listeners and rejects late callbacks and all subsequent controls. It does not own or disconnect a shared Epic client; that client must be stopped by its owner when appropriate.
- The local stage pauses on a hidden tab, respects reduced motion for automatic playback, and preserves app state if video never arrives or disconnects. Explicit Play/Replay remains available after genuine readiness. Retry replaces the iframe and nonce, advances the bridge session, and restores the last confirmed position paused. It requires new receiver/video proof; no playback callback awards a rep or resolves an exchange.

## Local browser boundary

The embedded page is fixed at `http://127.0.0.1:49080/jiuquest/embed`. Parent and child check the exact peer origin, window source, fresh per-mount UUID nonce and a bounded JSON-string envelope. Only protocol v1 scene commands, validated receiver responses, transport lifecycle and actual video-frame proof can cross that boundary. The frame CSP permits only the exact development app as an ancestor. Signalling remains on the original loopback ports, and the stock `/` frontend remains unchanged. Keyboard, mouse, touch, gamepad, camera and microphone capture are disabled in the embedded Epic client; app controls send the bounded JiuQuest protocol only.

Only advertised imported clips can play. The initial receiver targets `standing_stance`, `rear_naked_choke` and the generic `armbar` pilot; the armbar is not an exact guide mapping.
