# Unreal renderer pilot inside JiuQuest

The purchased-gi MetaHuman now has a separately verified local Win64 standalone idle/walk preview. It is not the paired-character Editor receiver described below and does not yet advertise combat moves. The two DGX Sparks have authenticated hardware inventories; their installed-software compatibility gaps and the remaining release checks are recorded in [Unreal Spark hosting](unreal-spark-hosting.md).

The experimental [native character receiver](../unreal/native-runtime/README.md) separates video from local playback commands. Its `jiuquest.characterPreview` channel is limited to the human idle/walk study. It is not connected to this app's combat bridge, and importing its source does not start a listener or change the production renderer. See its validation table for the exact compiled, tested and still-pending boundaries.

The local development app can select an Unreal Pixel Streaming preview in its actual Training and Arena views. This is restricted to a Vite development build at `http://127.0.0.1:5178`; use `?engine=unreal` or the local **Character graphics** selector. Production renderer selection is unchanged. The local iframe loads the pinned Epic UE5.8 library from the loopback service described in `docs/unreal-local-streaming.md`. This integration does not supply a public GPU host. A working Unreal receiver, actual imported characters, controls and presented video must be verified together; protocol tests alone do not prove rendering.

## Public character preview (captured stills)

jiuquest.com shows three captured stills of the saved purchased-gi character at `/?preview=unreal`, linked from the Overview. Per the local capture receipt recorded November 10, 2026, they are unedited native viewport captures from the verified character-control scene: white gi standing, a face and collar close-up, and navy gi during the walk animation. `src/unreal/characterPreview.js` pins each file's size and SHA-256, and `tests/unrealCharacterPreview.test.js` fails if a file is replaced or re-encoded. Change the pinned values only together with a newly verified capture.

The page is a static gallery. It never mounts the game, reads or writes saved progress, opens a stream, or changes renderer selection; the production combat renderer remains Unity. `tools/test_unreal_character_preview.mjs` checks the built page in a browser: image decoding, view selection, phone layouts and the absence of saved-progress writes. That check covers still images only. It is not evidence of Unreal rendering, browser video or paired choreography, which remain the separate runtime checks described below.

## Unreal athlete in the Fighter Creator (tintable stills)

The Fighter Creator offers two looks for a fighter: **Unreal athlete** and **Classic**. The Unreal athlete is the purchased-gi character as a front-view still. It appears on the profile stage, in the round portraits, beside the proficiency test in Training, on the rank path in Insights, and in the Overview hero when the hero is at least 700 pixels wide and the page is wider than 760 pixels (otherwise the hero shows the `standing_stance` poster instead; see the narrow hero below). Arena and Training position art are unchanged and still use the classic appearance choices.

The picture is four still layers that the browser adds together:

    picture = base + jacket x giTop + pants x giPants + belt x beltColor

The athlete stays dark until the body and all three gi layers have loaded, so a gi never shows without the person wearing it; a layer that fails to load does not hold the others back.

`tools/build_unreal_athlete_layers.py` derives the layers from three aligned renders in the local `Unreal-render-pack/athlete-01/front` set: white gi, black gi, and black gi with a white belt. The render receipt of November 11, 2026 records them as unedited 2400 by 3000 native renders of the hip-belt variant `BP_AthleteGi_HipBelt`, in which the belt sits on the hips. The published layers are 900 by 1760. They are derived images, not direct renders. With every color white the sum gives back the white-gi render (mean difference 0.013 of 255 on the gi). Every other color is computed rather than rendered, and there is no rendered reference for a jacket and pants in different colors, because the current materials color the whole gi at once; the tool separates the two at the jacket hem it finds in the white-gi render. The belt takes the rank color; stripes are not drawn on the athlete. The pack also holds side, back and portrait renders, which the site does not use yet.

`src/unreal/athlete.js` pins the SHA-256 of the three source renders and the size and SHA-256 of each published layer, and `tests/unrealAthlete.test.js` fails if a layer is replaced or re-encoded or if the tool and the page disagree about the sources. Replace the layers only by rerunning the tool on newly verified renders and updating the pinned values together. The first published layers (November 11, 2026, #24) came from four lower-resolution captures in `Unreal-gi-color-preview` with the belt at the waist; the still preview at `/?preview=unreal` still shows captures of that earlier variant.

There is one athlete: one face, build and hairstyle, in one pose. While it is selected the Fighter Creator hides gender, hair, skin tone and patch emblem, and keeps their saved values for the way back to Classic. New fighters and first visits start as the Unreal athlete. A fighter saved before this change moves to the Unreal athlete only if it still has the stock face, hair, skin and emblem; a fighter someone designed keeps its classic art until its owner switches.

`tools/test_unreal_athlete.mjs` checks the built site in a browser: layer decoding and blending, painted colors after each choice, both looks, saved fighters, reload, and 390 and 320 pixel layouts. Like the preview check, it covers still images only and is not evidence of Unreal rendering, browser video or paired choreography. Selecting the athlete never opens a stream and is not an input to renderer selection.

## Pre-rendered Unreal clips (Arena standing stance)

The Arena shows a pre-rendered Unreal clip while the position is Standing and no exchange result is on screen: before a match starts, through the first sequence of a standing start, and in any later round from the moment a technique is chosen until that exchange settles, if the position is still Standing. (A clinch is on the feet too, but it is its own position and shows no clip.) The clip follows the player's starting stance (see [Starting stances](unreal-stances.md)). `standing_stance` shows both athletes in a crouched wrestling stance, in side and front views; it was rendered again on November 13, 2026 to replace the earlier upright pose. Six `stance_<id>` clips show the player in a named stance, in the side view; sumo and sambo also have a front view. `stance_sumo` and `stance_sambo` also exist for a rival who holds that stance. Every clip is 1280 by 720, 30 frames a second, 54 frames, looping. While a settled exchange is on screen, and for every other position, the Arena shows the Unity animation where one exists, otherwise the position picture (see the position stills below).

### Technique clips

Every curriculum technique can play pre-rendered Unreal clips: all 49 in `allMoves` (`src/data/curriculum.js`), including the white-belt starters and the techniques Unity has no animation of. They switch over one technique at a time, as each technique's clips are accepted. A technique's clips go by its clip id, `unrealClipMoveId` in `src/unreal/techniqueIds.js`: a technique Unity animates keeps the id Unity gave it (`double_leg`, `rear_naked_choke`, `trap_roll_escape` and `triangle_choke`), and every other technique is its curriculum id with each `-` written as `_` (`americana-armlock` is `americana_armlock`, `headlock-escape-2` is `headlock_escape_2`). The clip id is the folder a technique's files are rendered into and published under (`<clip id>/<performer>-<camera>.mp4`) and the `moveId` its clips carry in the clip list. `chooseTechniquePicture` in `src/unreal/techniquePicture.js` decides by the clip id, and `GrapplingStage` follows it in Training (the player performs) and in the Arena (the winner of the exchange performs; `resolveArenaClipPlayback` gives the clip id and the performer):

- A technique plays its Unreal clips only once every performer the game can show has a published clip of it: both `fighter` and `opponent`, or the `fighter` alone for a technique the rival never performs from its clip start (`techniqueClipPerformers` and `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS` in `src/unreal/techniquePicture.js`, which `techniqueClipReady` follows). Training plays only the `fighter` clip, and the Arena plays an `opponent` clip only after the rival wins with the technique in an exchange that started at its clip start. The rival never does that with 15 techniques: the nine previews (`purple_omoplata`, `purple_darce`, `purple_x_guard_sweep`, `brown_pressure_pass`, `brown_knee_cut`, `brown_crucifix`, `black_berimbolo`, `black_leg_drag` and `black_bow_arrow`), because `chooseNpcMove` in `src/App.jsx` never gives the rival a preview technique above white belt, and six whose clip start the rival is never in, because no start and no technique ever puts the player on top there (`double_ankle_sweep`, `blocking_standing_punch_from_ground` and `hook_sweep` from `open-guard-bottom`; `headlock_scissors_choke` and `headlock_escape_2` from `headlock`; `standing_headlock_defense` from `standing-headlock`). Each of those switches over with its fighter clips alone; a rival's win with one, which the game never produces, would still show what it shows today, because a clip plays only for a performer with a view of their own. Every other technique needs clips of both performers. Until a technique switches over it plays exactly what it played before: for the four Unity animates, Unity on the release build and the position picture elsewhere; for every other technique, the position picture, headed "Position reference" in Training. Once a technique's clips play, its heading in Training is "Movement preview". A technique is never half Unreal and half Unity.
- `tests/unrealRivalReach.test.js` keeps that list honest by working it out again from the game's own rules. It walks every Arena state the game can reach from every start the Arena offers (Standing Start, Kneeling Start, each Position Start option and the profile's start poses). In each state the player may choose any curriculum technique offered from the player's side, and may win only where the counter rule lets it; the rival answers with any technique `chooseNpcMove` can return, for every rival belt and including its Protect Your Position fallback (the function is cut from `src/App.jsx` and run as written, and pinned line for line, so a change to the rival's rule fails the test); either side may win, and `resolveArenaOutcome` (`src/arena/outcome.js`) gives the next state. The rival performs a technique from its clip start when some state the game reaches puts the rival there with the technique offered to it. The test fails, naming the ids to add and remove, when `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS` is not exactly the techniques the rival never performs from their clip start, in curriculum order. It also asserts the converse: every technique's fighter clip can be seen, in Training (which lists 45 techniques) or, for the four white-belt starters Training does not list, after the player's Arena win from Standing. The search ignores scores and the round limit, so it allows more than the game does, and a clip it never asks for is never shown.
- A technique's clips are rendered from one position, its clip start: the first position it starts in (`move.starts[0]`, `unrealClipStart` in `src/unreal/techniqueIds.js`). Training shows every technique it lists in that position; it lists the blue to black belt techniques, so the clips of the four white-belt starters (`protect_position`, `basic_punch`, `basic_kick` and `basic_tackle`) play only in the Arena. In the Arena its clip plays only after an exchange that started there: the exchange result records where the exchange started, seen from the winner's side and in the position ids the game offers techniques by (`startedFrom`, `src/App.jsx`), and `resolveArenaClipPlayback` asks for the clip only when that is the winning technique's clip start. After any other start the exchange shows exactly what it shows without clips, and a result that does not say where it started gets no clip at all (the Arena then passes `GrapplingStage` a `clipMoveId` of null). This matters for the eight techniques that start in more than one position, each rendered from the first: `protect_position` (all 26 positions, `standing` first; it is also the rival's fallback move), `basic_punch` and `basic_tackle` (`standing`, then `kneeling`), `guillotine_choke` (`standing`, then `clinch`), `standing_headlock_defense` (`standing-headlock`, then `standing`), `guillotine_defense` (`clinch`, then `standing`), `purple_darce` (`side-top`, then `scramble`) and `brown_crucifix` (`back-control`, then `scramble`). The four Unity animates each start in one position.
- `?engine=snapshot` in the address turns technique clips off (position pictures only). `?engine=unity` keeps Unity for techniques until Unity is removed (a technique Unity does not animate shows its position picture there). The release default (`VITE_COMBAT_ENGINE=unity` with no parameter) is not an explicit choice, so clips win there.
- A move never uses another move's clip, the rival never gets the player's clip, and clips are never mirrored. The local live preview (`?engine=unreal`) never shows a clip.
- A technique clip plays once, rests on its last frame and offers Replay. Its autoplay waits until half the picture is on screen, it starts paused under reduced motion, and it pauses while the tab is hidden. Training adds 0.25×, 0.5× and 1× speeds and a frame scrubber. If a view fails, the stage tries the other camera, then hands over to the Unity stage or the position picture. In Training the heading then follows the picture left on screen: the clip stage tells `GrapplingStage` (`onClipUnavailable`), and Training works the heading out as if that clip were not published, so a technique Unity does not animate is headed "Position reference" again, while the four Unity animates keep "Movement preview" over the Unity stage on the release build. Opening another technique forgets it, so coming back tries the clip again.

No technique clip is published yet, so every technique still plays as before. The `double_leg` takes in the November 13, 2026 pack are drafts and stay held. `tools/test_unreal_technique_clips.mjs` checks the technique stage in test bundles whose clip list relabels published stance files as `double_leg`, which Unity animates, as `establish_clinch`, which it does not and which also starts standing, and as `basic_tackle`, which starts standing (its clip start) or kneeling; those bundles are never published. One bundle has the clips of both performers, the other the fighter's only: with those alone the double leg must stay on Unity and `establish_clinch` on the position picture, because both need the rival's clips too. The fighter-only bundle also gives `hook_sweep` (Hook sweep takedown), which is in `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS`, its fighter clips, and the suite checks that they switch it over: Training plays its clip under "Movement preview" (the whole study stage), and the player's Arena wins from a Position Start in Open Guard From Bottom, its clip start, play the player's clip. Besides the stage itself, it checks that Training shows `establish_clinch` exactly as the release build without clips does when none of its views can play, and plays the clip again once the technique is opened anew; that a Mat Tackle win plays the winner's clip after a Standing Start and the position picture after a Kneeling Start; and, by measuring the boxes at 1440, 390 and 320 pixels wide, that every colour dot in a legend sits beside its own text, also where a label wraps.

`node tools/build_technique_sheet.mjs <output dir>` writes the technique sheet the renderer works from, `TECHNIQUE-SHEET.md` and `TECHNIQUE-SHEET.json`: every technique in curriculum order under its clip id, with its curriculum id, name, belt, lesson (for a blue-belt technique, the approved guide's starting position instead), category, scenario, starting and ending positions, whether it is a submission, its numbered steps, whether Unity has an animation of it ("Unity animation", a fact about Unity: the site plays that animation until the technique's Unreal clips switch on) and which Unreal clip views are published today. Names, lessons, scenarios and steps are copied character for character from `src/data` and never corrected. Its header counts the techniques, the steps, those with a Unity animation, those playing Unreal clips today by the rule above, and how many need clips of both performers and how many the fighter's only. Each technique's section says whose clips it needs, by the site's own rule (`techniqueClipPerformers`): "Clips needed: fighter and opponent.", or "Clips needed: fighter only." with the reason ("The rival never uses preview techniques", or for example "The rival is never in Open Guard From Bottom in the game", naming its Render from position) and that an opponent clip would never be shown; the index has the same in its "Clips needed" column. The next line says which view the site opens the technique on ("The site opens on"), read from `TECHNIQUE_OPENING_CAMERA` in `src/unreal/techniquePicture.js` as `chooseTechniquePicture` reads it: for `rear_naked_choke` the front view once one is published and the side view until then, and for every other technique the side view, or the front view where that is the only one. The section of each white-belt starter says that Training does not list it, so its clips play only in the Arena. Each technique's section also names the position it is rendered from ("Render from", its clip start) and, where the game offers it from more than one position, the others ("The game also offers it from", after which the site shows the position picture, not the clip). The sheet works out with the game's own rules (`src/arena/outcome.js`) where the next exchange starts after each performer wins from the Render from position, with the other athlete playing Protect Your Position (a counter against the attack it answers), and adds "In the game the next exchange starts from" wherever that is not the technique's "Ends in"; today that is so for no technique. Its "Clip files" section takes `rear_naked_choke`, the first technique to be rendered, as the example. It says that every clip starts in the Render from position and ends on the finish its approved steps describe: for a technique that is not a submission, that finish is its "Ends in" position, where the game continues; a submission does not move the athletes in the game, so when it does not end the match the next exchange starts again from its Render from position, and its clip still ends on the approved finish. It explains whose clips each technique needs and when the site switches a technique over, and lists what the importer requires of every delivered clip: an entry in the pack's `clips/technique-clips.json` whose size and SHA-256 match the file, `"loops": false` and `"draft": false`, a same-name `.webp` poster with the video's pixel size (for a technique clip the site cuts its own poster from frame 0, but the pack's poster must exist), H.264 High, `yuv420p`, no sound and the index before the media, frames and fps that match the duration, the same frame count for every view of one performer, and an acceptance line in `tools/unreal_clip_acceptance.json`, which Claude writes after reviewing the clip. It adds that a delivered entry that is not a draft and fails any of these checks, other than a missing acceptance line, stops the whole import, so no clip in the pack is published until it is fixed or marked `"draft": true` (the importer checks every entry that is not a draft as it reads the list, and a clip's own files once the clip has its acceptance line), and asks for a take that is not ready to be delivered with `"draft": true`. The JSON gives the same for each technique as `renderFrom`, `otherStarts`, `gameContinuesFrom`, `clipsNeeded` (from `techniqueClipPerformers`), `fighterOnlyReason` (the reason, or null), `clipFilesNeeded` (the file names of the performers in `clipsNeeded` only, in the order of `clipFileNames`, which still lists every name the importer accepts) and `opensOn` (`front` or `side`), the last four after every earlier key; its notice says what `clipFileNames` and `clipFilesNeeded` each mean. The sheet names the commit it was made from and says when the files it is made from have uncommitted changes, so make it after the release commit. `tests/techniqueSheet.test.js` checks it against the curriculum, the game's rules, the site's rules for whose clips a technique needs (and that each reason is true in the game) and for the view it opens a technique on, and the importer, including that a failed check stops the whole import while a draft or a clip without its acceptance line is only left out.

`node tools/import_unreal_clips.mjs <Unreal-render-pack/clips> <ffmpeg bin dir>` copies each MP4 from the render pack unchanged, after checking it against the pack's `technique-clips.json` (size and SHA-256) and with ffprobe (H.264 High, yuv420p, picture size, frame rate, frame count, index before media, no audio). It writes a smaller lossy WebP poster beside each one with ffmpeg's libwebp (quality 82): from the pack's lossless poster for a looping clip, and from frame 0 of the video for a clip that plays once (`"posterFrame": 0`). It writes `src/unreal/techniqueClips.data.js`, which pins every published file. A technique clip, one whose move is the clip id of any curriculum technique (`TECHNIQUE_IDS`, all 49), must have `"loops": false` and `"draft": false`, the same frame count in every view of one performer, and an owner's acceptance line in `tools/unreal_clip_acceptance.json` (`{ "file", "sha256", "acceptedOn" }`) for that exact file; without one it is left out and listed under `notAccepted`. That rule is the same for every technique, and the acceptance list is still empty. Stance clips need no acceptance line. A move name is lower-case letters, digits and underscores, starting with a letter, and a move that is neither a technique's clip id nor a stance clip is refused. `--out <dir>` writes everything under another folder instead, for a dry run. The importer is Node because the machine that imports has no Python; it replaced `tools/import_unreal_clips.py`, and re-running it on the November 13, 2026 pack publishes the same files byte for byte. `tests/unrealTechniqueClips.test.js` fails if a file is replaced, if a clip names neither a curriculum technique's clip id nor a stance clip, or if a file is published without being pinned.

The outfits in a clip are fixed: the player's fighter in a white gi and the opponent in navy, both in black belts, whatever the player's own fighter wears. The stage says so beside the picture. Both athletes are the same character. The pack manifest, updated November 13, 2026, records for every published clip that its author played the encoded file at normal and quarter speed, and that no qualified choreography review has been done (`choreographyReviewed` is false).

`tools/test_unreal_clip_stage.mjs` checks the built site in a browser: the stance clip plays, loops, pauses and changes camera; reduced motion starts paused on the poster; a clip that cannot load gives way to the position snapshot; the stance gives way once an exchange settles; 390 and 320 pixel layouts. It also walks every technique: one with published clips of every performer it needs (both, or the fighter alone for a technique in `FIGHTER_ONLY_CLIP_IDS`) is checked in Training and in a player's Arena win against the real files, and every other one must show what it showed before on the release default, the Unity stage for the four Unity animates and the position picture for the rest, in Training, or in the Arena for the white-belt starters Training does not list. It covers a video file played by the browser. It is not evidence of Unreal rendering in the browser or of a stream. The local live preview (`?engine=unreal` on localhost) is unchanged and renders the stance itself.

## Unreal position stills (standing, tintable)

Where the Arena and Training show a picture of a settled position, the site can show a player who chose the Unreal athlete two Unreal athletes for the standing position, each in their own jacket, pants and belt colors: in Training as the position reference for techniques that start standing, in the Arena when an exchange settles with both athletes on their feet, and in the Overview's scenario teaser. Every other position shows the classic picture, because Unreal has not rendered it yet.

**Shown since the belt-tail repair.** The still shows both athletes crouched in a wrestling stance. It is frame 0 of the crouched `standing_stance` clip (`standing_stance/fighter-side.mp4`), from the side camera at x=560, so it is the pose the Arena's standing clip starts from. In the first crouched renders, delivered earlier on November 13, 2026, a patch of the lead athlete's belt tail showed on the back of his near thigh, so the still was held back. Unreal rendered the five passes again at 15:34 that day with that patch removed: same camera, frame and pose, and only the lead's belt-tail mesh changed. Those renders are built into the published layers, the jacket outlines still fit the pose in the tool's `--debug` pictures, and composites in several gi and belt colors show no patch on the thigh, so `HELD_UNREAL_POSITION_STILLS` in `src/unreal/positionStills.js` is empty and the still is shown. Codex notes that the free end of the tail is mostly hidden behind the athlete, so whether it clears the body out of sight is not verified, and that the accepted 720p standing clip still contains the original patch. To hold a still back again, put its position key in that list (every player then sees the classic picture and downloads no layer) and update the hold-back test in `tests/unrealPositionStills.test.js`. (Before this, a first still with both athletes standing upright was published on November 11, 2026 and held back on November 13, because the owner wants standing athletes crouched.)

Unreal rendered the frame five times at 2560 by 1440: everything black, each athlete's gi in white, each athlete's belt in white. `tools/build_unreal_position_layers.mjs` checks each render against the pack's `positions.json` (size and SHA-256), cuts out the three-to-two stage, splits each gi into jacket and pants, and writes seven lossy WebP layers plus `src/unreal/positionStills.data.js`, which pins every published file and the hashes of the renders. The crop is 1620 by 1080 render pixels with its top left corner at 510, 255, and it is published at that size, one render pixel per published pixel. The seven files total about 98 kB. The browser adds the layers:

`picture = base + leadJacket x lead.giTop + leadPants x lead.giPants + leadBelt x lead belt + the same three for the other athlete`

With a white gi the layers give back the white-gi render, away from the belt, to a mean error of 0.03 out of 255. "Lead" is the athlete on top or in control; in a neutral position it is the athlete on the left, the player.

Where a belt's edge lies over its athlete's gi, the gi pass and the belt pass both add light to the same pixels, and simply adding the two layers showed a white belt on a white gi with a bright line along the belt's edge. The tool takes the excess out of the gi layer, pixel by pixel, so that base plus gi plus belt is never brighter than the two white renders added in linear light (each counted only where it is brighter than the black render, as the layers are). That is what a pixel that is part gi and part belt shows when both are white. An earlier cap at the brighter of the two white renders also removed the bright line, but on a pixel the belt's edge half covers, each white render is only half bright, so that cap left a one-pixel dark line along a light belt on a light gi; the linear-light cap leaves neither. With every color white, the edge pixels match the linear-light estimate of an all-white render to a median of under one level; isolated pixels differ by up to about 25, mostly WebP error on the belt layer's sharp edge, and where the render itself shades the belt's edge a one-pixel dip remains. A dark belt on a light gi comes out up to about one pixel crisper at its edges than Codex's render, because the trim takes the gi's share out of a half-covered edge pixel and a dark belt adds little back; at the sizes the site shows the picture that is under half a display pixel.

Unreal colors a whole gi at once, so the tool decides where the jacket ends and the pants begin. For an upright athlete it finds the hem by itself: inside a window, column by column, the darkest row under the skirt (`hem` in the tool's `POSITIONS`). The crouched pose defeats that. Each jacket hangs over a bent hip, the corner of the skirt hangs free beside the hip, and pants show between that corner and the belt, so some columns read jacket, pants, jacket, pants from top to bottom and no single hem row separates them. For this still each athlete's jacket is instead an outline traced by hand in render pixels (`jacket` in `POSITIONS`). It follows the shadow line under the hem, the edges of the skirt corner and the front edge of the skirt up to the belt; everywhere else it runs through background or through the middle of the belt. On the other athlete the dark line up the free corner of the skirt is an edge over the pants only in its lower part: it fades as it climbs and runs on into the jacket, so it is a fold. The outline leaves it where it is still dark and runs straight up into the rolled front edge of the skirt, so the cloth above the fold is jacket (an earlier outline cut straight across that cloth and left a notch of pants under a ledge of jacket). Gi pixels inside the outline are jacket and the rest are pants, blended over four pixels, the same width as the hem blend. The tool's `--debug` pictures paint the jacket blue and the pants red; both were looked at, at full size, before these layers were published.

The tool exists twice, with the same arithmetic and the same `POSITIONS`: `tools/build_unreal_position_layers.mjs` (Node, with sharp for WebP) and `tools/build_unreal_position_layers.py` (Pillow, NumPy, SciPy). The `.mjs` is the tool of record on a machine without Python, and it built the layers published now: `node tools/build_unreal_position_layers.mjs <Unreal-render-pack/positions> --debug <folder>`. Change both files together. Before the Node version was trusted, it was run on the five renders of the upright still with the settings for that still, and it reproduced all seven published WebP files and the data module byte for byte. The outline method and the belt overlap trim were then added to both, with the same numbers; the Python version of those additions has not been run. The bytes of a WebP file depend on the libwebp release, so the two are not expected to write identical files.

`chooseUnrealPositionStill` decides between the still and the classic picture. The still is used where it shows the exact position (the neutral standing stance). It also stands in, labeled as a general reference, where the classic picture was already only a general reference (rear clinch, standing headlock). It is not used where the classic picture shows the exact setup and the still does not: the clinch, whose picture shows the grips, and the Position Lab, whose text describes a grip exchange. A fighter with the classic look keeps the classic pictures and downloads no still. If any layer fails to load, the stage shows the classic picture and never a half-colored athlete.

Limits: both athletes are the same character, belts show no rank stripes, and the pose has had no owner or coach review (`poseReviewed` is false). The outlines are one person's reading of where each jacket ends, and where the skirt lies flat against the pants the edge in the render is faint. `tests/unrealPositionStills.test.js` pins the files and the selection rule, checks that the still is built from the belt-tail repair renders, and checks that nothing is held back. `tools/test_unreal_position_still.mjs` checks the built site in a browser. While a still is held back it checks that a player with the Unreal look gets the classic picture in Training, the Overview teaser and the Arena, and downloads no still file. When the still is shown it checks instead that the still appears in those places, the classic picture stays where it shows the exact setup and for a fighter with the classic look, the painted colors match the layer arithmetic at seven places on the picture (each gi half, each belt and a cheek), each of those places lies in its own layer and in no other (so a swapped, emptied or misplaced layer fails even when the browser adds it correctly), a layer that cannot load brings back the classic picture, and the page fits 390 and 320 pixel phones. ## Epic frontend boundary

The injected interface matches Epic's [UE5.8 PixelStreaming frontend implementation](https://github.com/EpicGamesExt/PixelStreamingInfrastructure/blob/UE5.8/Frontend/library/src/PixelStreaming/PixelStreaming.ts), inspected on 2026-11-08:

- `emitUIInteraction(descriptor)` accepts an object and serializes it; `false` means it could not send. The adapter passes an object, not an already serialized string.
- `addResponseEventListener(name, listener)` receives Unreal response strings; `removeResponseEventListener(name)` removes that listener.
- `addEventListener` and `removeEventListener` register lifecycle callbacks. The adapter invalidates its session on `webRtcDisconnected`, `webRtcFailed`, `streamPreDisconnect`, or `streamReconnect`.

No `emitCommand`, console command or arbitrary URL is exposed. The local frontend uses infrastructure commit `4afd64170216002070a42fa52a87a44d4e97e77c`, matching UE5.8, checked by the loopback launcher. Package and interface checks do not establish that imported assets render correctly.

The caller owns the Epic client, connection, video element and teardown. Create the bridge with a fresh unique ID for each mounted surface (for example `crypto.randomUUID()`). After the client's data channel can send, call `attach(client)`. A send rejection immediately returns the bridge to offline. Retry by attaching after the actual connection is usable, rather than treating a socket connection as success.

```js
const bridge = createUnrealBridge({
  instanceId: crypto.randomUUID(),
  onStatus: updatePlaybackConnectionState,
  onEvent: updatePlaybackOnly,
});
bridge.setScene({
  moveId: 'double_leg',
  performerSide: 'opponent',
  fighter, fighterBelt,
  opponent, opponentBelt,
  speed: 1, camera: 'side', compactViewport: false,
}, false);
// After a real Epic client has a usable data channel:
bridge.attach(client);
// On unmount: bridge.dispose(), then the owner tears down its Epic client.
```

This illustrates the bridge used by `UnrealGrapplingStage`. Its callbacks only update connection/playback UI. The iframe reports an open data channel only after Epic's actual `isDataChannelOpen()` returns true. After each `play`, it waits for a matching receiver `state` acknowledgement and two subsequent `requestVideoFrameCallback` presentations with nonzero video dimensions. The parent accepts that proof only for its current play request. A 45-second initial deadline prevents an endless startup state. `receiverReady` alone does not enable the stage controls or prove visible video, correct assets, quality or performance.

## Receiver protocol, version 1

Every command and response uses this envelope:

```json
{
  "channel": "jiuquest.combat",
  "protocolVersion": 1,
  "instanceId": "unique-mounted-surface",
  "sessionId": "unique-mounted-surface:1",
  "requestId": "unique-mounted-surface:1:setup",
  "type": "configure"
}
```

`attach` advances the session. Only `configure` is sent before the receiver handshake. The receiver must echo the instance, session and setup request and advertise only assets actually imported and ready in that runtime:

```json
{
  "channel": "jiuquest.combat",
  "protocolVersion": 1,
  "instanceId": "unique-mounted-surface",
  "sessionId": "unique-mounted-surface:1",
  "requestId": "unique-mounted-surface:1:setup",
  "type": "ready",
  "capabilities": { "moves": ["standing_stance", "double_leg"] }
}
```

There are 16 recognized canonical IDs in `UNREAL_MOVE_IDS`; recognition does not assert Unreal support. Only advertised IDs can play. The existing four curriculum mappings remain exact: `trap-roll-escape`, `double-leg-takedown`, `triangle-choke`, and `rear-naked-choke`. A generic armbar or pass must not stand in for an unmatched lesson. An empty capability list is valid but enables no playback controls.

The latest queued scene plays after a valid handshake. It contains:

| Field | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `moveId` | Recognized and advertised canonical technique |
| `performerSide` | `fighter` or `opponent`; maps actor identity, not match result |
| `fighter`, `opponent` | Name, skin/hair colors, hair style, gi top/pants colors, belt color, stripes 0–4 only |
| `progress` | Finite number 0–1, shared by both actors |
| `paused` | Boolean |
| `speed` | 0.25, 0.5 or 1 |
| `camera` | `front`, `focus`, `side`, `overhead` or `orbit` |
| `compactViewport` | Boolean composition hint |

After readiness, the controls are `pause`, `resume`, `seek`, `reset`, `setSpeed`, `setCamera`, `setViewport`, and `requestState`. Controls carry the full sanitized scene snapshot and a new request ID; only `play` should replace/load a clip. Seek and reset pause; reset seeks to zero. Appearance changes use `setScene`, which sends a new `play` snapshot. No separate appearance or actor-clock command exists. A known but unadvertised selection pauses the previous scene and disables controls until a supported selection is made.

The receiver sends `state` and `completed` with the exact current request ID, current move ID, progress and playing boolean. A completion must report progress 1 and playing false. `error` has a plain message of 1–240 characters and optionally the active move ID; a configure error uses the setup request. Errors take the bridge offline. Stale sessions, stale requests, wrong moves and protocol versions are ignored. The receiver must similarly discard superseded asynchronous loads and commands from prior sessions.

All responses are JSON strings limited to 8,192 UTF-8 bytes. IDs are bounded and contain only letters, digits, colon, underscore and hyphen. Outbound values are explicitly selected; extra renderer fields cannot propagate scores or persistence actions to the app. The Unreal receiver must independently validate the same schema and restrict operations to this command list. These bounds are not session authentication: the hosting layer must authorize and isolate clients.

## Disconnect and lifecycle behavior

- A missing application handshake times out after 15 seconds. Transport connectivity never enables controls by itself.
- Visibility/reduced-motion pauses and viewport hints received before readiness update the queued scene without sending a control early or resetting saved playback progress.
- Stream loss, rejected sends and receiver errors disable controls and freeze the last confirmed progress with playing false.
- Reattach changes the session, repeats the handshake and sends the latest scene paused at its last confirmed position. It does not resolve another turn or award a rep.
- Dispose removes response/lifecycle listeners and rejects late callbacks and all subsequent controls. It does not own or disconnect a shared Epic client; that client must be stopped by its owner when appropriate.
- The local stage pauses on a hidden tab, respects reduced motion for automatic playback, and preserves app state if video never arrives or disconnects. Explicit Play/Replay remains available after genuine readiness. Retry replaces the iframe and nonce, advances the bridge session, and restores the last confirmed position paused. It requires new receiver/video proof; no playback callback awards a rep or resolves an exchange.

## Local browser boundary

The embedded page is fixed at `http://127.0.0.1:49080/jiuquest/embed`. Parent and child check the exact peer origin, window source, fresh per-mount UUID nonce and a bounded JSON-string envelope. Only protocol v1 scene commands, validated receiver responses, transport lifecycle and actual video-frame proof can cross that boundary. The frame CSP permits only the exact development app as an ancestor. Signalling remains on the original loopback ports, and the stock `/` frontend remains unchanged. Keyboard, mouse, touch, gamepad, camera and microphone capture are disabled in the embedded Epic client; app controls send the bounded JiuQuest protocol only.

Only advertised imported clips can play. The initial receiver targets `standing_stance`, `rear_naked_choke` and the generic `armbar` pilot; the armbar is not an exact guide mapping. A public runtime, authenticated session allocation, hosted stream URL, production engine switch and additional hairstyles are outside this local mode.
